# Mixed Company — Walkthrough

1. On page 1 ("Mixed Company"), the Worked example's Given rigid pieces shows three bent pairs of panels, each with
a 90° bend label. The arrow leads to two views of the same closed Given target, a slanted box measured 2, 3 and 1,
and then to the Result. This establishes preserving the given shapes during assembly; the paired target drawings
are observations, not extra material.

2. Compare the original folds. The left piece's bend is drawn as a dash-dot line, while the bends of the middle and
right pieces carry scissors. In the Result, the panels on either side of a scissored bend are no longer attached
there. Thus a scissors mark cuts even a connection that belonged to an original piece. The original rigidity
describes its supplied shape, not an exemption from the later seam operations. Keeping those bends intact would
contradict the visible Result.

3. Follow the ink. Fragments of the letters lie on different pieces: parts of A and R sit near the edges of separate
pieces. In the Result they meet across joins of the box that were never bends of a single piece, and only then form
whole letters. Consequently a target hinge can connect panels from different source pieces. Following only the
original joins would not reproduce this result.

4. The target views mark every cut with scissors on a dashed seam. The caption states the rule: "Cut the marked seams;
other joins stay connected." The Result is exactly the box with the marked seams cut and every other edge kept as a
hinge. There is no unspecified choice of where to cut.

5. The ink remains on its panels. "Open flat with the arrow upright": with the material arrow upward, A, R and T
lie on one row, and the reading is positional, left to right: ART, rather than rearranging fragments into RAT or TAR.
Ordinary upright letter shapes confirm the reading side; no face fill encodes a forbidden side.

6. Page 2 says "Four rigid pieces, each shown twice". The eight drawings carry no labels and are not placed in pairs,
and the two views of a piece are taken from unrelated directions, so one cannot be obtained by mirroring the other.
Match vertices, panel boundaries, triangles and bends to recover four shapes. Number the drawings in reading order
(left then right, row by row). The only consistent matching is 1 with 6, 2 with 5, 3 with 8 and 4 with 7: the top-left
drawing goes with the third-row right one, the top-right with the third-row left, the second-row left with the bottom
right, and the second-row right with the bottom left. Short grey dashes are hidden edges, not a material-side code.
Every surface is white. The black arrow (in the bottom-left drawing) belongs to its panel.

7. Page 3 has four views of the same target, the Cuts legend, an End outline and a Puzzle word box showing only "?"

extrusion. Taking the end square's side as 2, the far end rises 3 and is displaced 1 in each horizontal direction, so
the shell is oblique in both horizontal directions. A right prism would disagree with the views. Each side face is
split by a diagonal into two triangles; the solid has 6 end squares and 16 side triangles, 22 panels in all.

8. Fit each complete bent shape to these panels. One rotation and translation must fit all its squares, triangles and
bends simultaneously, and the four pieces together must cover the shell without overlap. Both diagonal directions on

is unique without requiring a white-outside/grey-inside convention.

9. At every target seam, read the scissors from the four views. A seam hidden in one view is visible in another.

preserving their measured shapes; no new cut or hinge is chosen. The remaining joins connect the whole sheet.
